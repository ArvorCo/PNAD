**Two things you should know**
- **The `TOTC &lt;= APTA` rule you specified is wrong for 2026.** The Senate elects 2 seats, so each voter votes twice. If an urna writes r and s at a fixed 66 bytes, about 44% of its signatures would be non-minimal. **Algorithm details I had to pin down**
- **Hash chain:**
  - The hashed "dados" excludes the `QRBU:i:n VRQR:6.0` header.
  - The space removed at the split must be added back: all 10 examples need it, and without it the hash fails.
  - Because the chain is cumulative, changing QR k breaks links k through n, not just k.
- **Signed message:** SHA-512 of the 64 bytes decoded from the last HASH hex, not of the hex text.
- **ECDSA:** the signature is DER of 139 bytes (`30 81 88`, then r and s of 66 bytes each). The message is used as-is with no second hash, and high s is accepted, as in ECPy.
- **Ed521:**
  - **Encoding:** a point is 66 bytes, y in little-endian with x's parity in bit 0x80 of the last byte. The signature is R (66 bytes) followed by S (66 bytes, little-endian).
  - **Challenge:** h = SHAKE256("SigEd521" + 0x00 0x00 + R + A + message, 132 bytes), read little-endian and reduced mod n.
  - **Check:** R + h·A == S·B, without the cofactor.
  - **Why custom code:** noble's built-in EdDSA always multiplies by the cofactor, so I use noble's Edwards point arithmetic with my own check.
  - **Stricter than ECPy:** I require S &lt; n, y &lt; p, and reject a small-order key. An honest signer always meets these.

- **Certificate chain:**
  - In all four certificates, the issuer name bytes are identical to the AC's subject and the authority key ID equals the AC's key ID. Both are now required.
